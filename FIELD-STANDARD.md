# The Field Standard — the investigation bar for concepts

*Anchored 2026-07-05 on Frank's steer. Binding for every FLAGSHIP concept; the aspiration for
all. Source: Frank's field-map `FIELD.md` (github.com/frankbueltge/field-research) — the live
topology of where data, AI and power meet. Read this before pitching a flagship; the gate runs
against it (see PROTOCOL.md "The gate").*

## Why this exists

Session 6 came back with six flagship loglines — money creation, the birth lottery, tax
havens, the wage gap, the billionaire's wealth clock, the stuck social elevator. Frank's
verdict: *"alle eher schwach und nicht wirklich originell, progressiv oder investigativ … das
weiß doch schon jeder und es ist kein Geheimnis."* He is right, and the reason is structural,
not a bad-day fluke: **the table keeps pitching KNOWN PHENOMENA to explain, when a flagship
must be a SPECIFIC SYSTEM to investigate.** This standard exists so that stops happening at the
source — so surface Dünnpfiff dies at the pitch, not after Frank has read six of them.

## The Master Axis (from FIELD.md)

One opposition structures the whole field:

```
  SPECTACLE / EXPLAINER  ───────────────────────  INVESTIGATION / EVIDENCE
  (data as MEDIUM)                                  (data as SUBJECT)
  "here is a big true thing,                        a specific NAMED system, taken apart;
   made beautiful/visceral"                          the form IS the demonstration
  → everyone already knows it                        → "I didn't know that"
```

FIELD.md's finding: prestige, funding and relevance have moved decisively to the
**investigation pole**. Pure data-sublime is now read *inside institutions* as "a tech demo
disguised as art." **data-snack flagships live on the investigation pole.** Cookie Roulette is
already there — it doesn't explain "tracking is bad," it makes named corporate data-personae
gamble with *you*. Das Doppelte Gericht is there too (court-grade, named LG-Bonn anchors) — it
failed on internationality and comprehension, **not** on investigation. The rent snack and the
S6 six failed here: they *confirm* what the audience already knows.

## What is fading (do not pitch these as flagships — FIELD.md, verbatim targets)

- **The "What is X?" explainer survey show** — "audiences are no longer naive." Explaining a
  known mechanism (how banks create money, why heirs win) is an explainer, not an investigation.
- **Decorative dataviz-as-art** — must now "carry argumentative weight," not just render a
  true-but-known stat beautifully.
- **Abstract framing** — "surveillance capitalism," "inequality," "AI is coming" — **replaced
  by NAMED systems: Lavender, Gospel, the VW 800k-EV location leak.** Name the instrument.

If a logline could be the title of a decent explainer video that already exists on YouTube,
it is an Appetizer or a Quick Snack — **not a flagship.** Route it there and say so.

## The five-part originality test (a flagship must pass ALL)

A flagship concept graduates only if it clears every one. The Field-Checker runs this at the
gate; the pitching host runs it on itself first.

1. **Named specific subject, not a known phenomenon.** The subject is a *specific, named*
   system, instrument, dataset, ruling, or documented case — not a general truth. Litmus: would
   a well-read person say **"I didn't know that,"** or **"everyone knows that"?** "Banks create
   money" = everyone knows. "The AI tool your university uses to detect cheating flags
   non-native English speakers at 2–5× the rate, and students have been expelled and had visas
   revoked on its say-so" = a named system with a hidden, documented finding.
2. **Discovery, not confirmation.** It reveals something hidden, counter-intuitive, or
   suppressed that primary-source investigation surfaces — it does not restate common knowledge
   with better production. (data-snack's own oldest rule, sharpened.)
3. **Form enacts the argument.** The interaction *is* the demonstration — the player performs
   the mechanism and feels it (the site's snack test: *körperlich oder spielerisch erfahren*).
   The subject is the infrastructure/instrument, **not a chart of its output.**
4. **Method verifiable.** Primary, named, retrievable sources — the same bar as the fact gate.
   A flagship's shock must survive a Verifier who pulls the ruling, the paper, the leak.
5. **A secret with a victim.** The best data-snacks reveal a specific harm done to a specific
   party by a specific mechanism — not a diffuse "the system is unfair." Who is hurt, by which
   named thing, in a way they can't see? If you can't name the victim and the instrument, it's
   an essay, not an operation.

## The benchmark roster (what "form enacts the argument" looks like — FIELD.md)

The yardstick practices, and the move that separates them from "data made beautiful":

- **Forensic Architecture** — investigative aesthetics: the *form is the epistemological
  demonstration*; deployed in actual courtrooms.
- **Crawford & Joler, *Anatomy of an AI / Calculating Empires*** — the diagram *is* the thesis
  (recursive extraction), not an illustration of it.
- **Mimi Onuoha, *Library of Missing Datasets*** — absence as data; the empty folder *is* the
  claim.
- **Tega Brain, *Synthetic Messenger / Asunder*** — "eccentric engineering": a *working system*
  performs the critique from inside itself.
- **Critical Engineering (Julian Oliver)** — "the exploit as the most desirable form of
  exposure." (This is the data-snack move: let the player run the exploit.)
- **SITU Research** — visual investigation → ICC conviction. The most concrete evidence path.
- **Counter-benchmark — Refik Anadol / Dataland** — the spectacle pole; "empty cathedrals where
  algorithms replace thought." *What not to be.*

## FIELD.md's instrument series — a worked model of flagship-grade findings

FIELD.md's own "instrument series" is exactly the caliber this standard demands — specific,
named, investigated, non-obvious, with victims. Each is a measurement/detection system that
fails in a documented, counter-intuitive way. **These are not a mine to strip for data-snack
(they are Frank's research, and several are AI/tech, not the cast's economic core) — they are a
CALIBRATION SAMPLE: this is what "kein Geheimnis das jeder kennt" feels like.**

- **AI text detectors** punish non-native English speakers (false-positive rates 30–61% above
  baseline) — with real expelled students, revoked visas, pending lawsuits (Rignol v. Yale;
  Yang v. U. Minnesota). *Named tool, hidden calibration, named victims.*
- **COMPAS** recidivism scoring: ProPublica's "biased" and Northpointe's "fair" are
  *simultaneously true* — Chouldechova (2017) proved no classifier can satisfy both when base
  rates differ. The fairness criterion is *mathematically impossible*, not miscalibrated.
- **C2PA** content-provenance: forgeable via `c2patool` (any image signed as any creator,
  "no evidence of tampering"); and the device certificates *de-anonymize journalists*. The
  authenticity standard breaks exactly at the highest-stakes node.
- **The unifying finding** across seven instruments: *a tool's strongest guarantee coincides
  with its lowest-stakes use; its weakest guarantee with its highest-stakes use.* That single
  non-obvious law is worth more than any "inequality is bad" logline.

The common shape to steal: **take a named system the audience trusts or takes for granted, and
show — by making them operate it — the specific, sourced way it betrays a specific party.**

## How this binds

- **At the pitch (self-check):** a flagship logline that fails part 1 or 2 of the test does not
  reach the table. The pitching host says, in one line, the named subject and the hidden finding.
  "Everyone knows that" is a kill at the pitch.
- **At the gate (Field-Checker):** for any concept graduating `drafts/` → `works/` as a
  **flagship**, an ephemeral **Field-Checker** reads it against this standard (the five-part
  test) alongside the Verifier and Voice-Checker. All three must pass. (Appetizers and Quick
  Snacks are *allowed* to restate a known fact well — that's their job; the bar here is for
  flagships.)
- **The honest routing valve:** a concept that fails this bar is not worthless — it is an
  **Appetizer, a Quick Snack, or an Atlas.** Say which, and route it there. "Everyone knows it"
  is a real, useful thing to serve — just not as Rook's next blockbuster.

## The one-line version

> A flagship is not a beautiful explanation of something everyone knows. It is a specific,
> named system taken apart with your own hands, revealing a sourced secret with a victim.
